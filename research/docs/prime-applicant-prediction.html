§ 1

Where the model sits in the money

The Hawaii win is the whole thesis in one sentence: Healthy Mothers Healthy Babies won the Department of Health RFP, and Avocado was the platform underneath. The unit of pursuit is therefore never the solicitation. It is the organization that is about to have money and will need something to deliver with.

That makes prediction tractable, because the cascade is public. Every layer below is readable from federal award records, state plan documents and procurement portals.

§ 2

Three models, because it is three questions

A single score collapses three independent uncertainties and hides which one is driving the answer. Separating them means each can be validated against its own evidence, and a target that ranks low can be diagnosed rather than merely discarded.

Model A — will this organization apply and win?

Learning-to-rank, not binary classification. The operational question is never "will this org win money" in the abstract; it is "for this solicitation, which twenty organizations should get contact mapping this week." LightGBM LambdaRank with query groups set to solicitations and NDCG truncated at 30 optimises exactly the part of the list a human reads.

Labels are graded: 2 for a prime awardee, 1 for a first-tier subrecipient, 0 for eligible-and-not-selected, measured over an eighteen-month window after close. The grading is deliberate. A first-tier subrecipient is often the better Avocado prospect: it has money, it has delivery obligations, and it is small enough to need a partner.

Negatives are the eligible universe, not a random sample. Random negatives teach the model "is this org even in the right state," which is trivially true at scan time and destroys the signal where it matters.

Model B — is this a viable Avocado deployment?

Positive-unlabelled learning, because Avocado has roughly five known positives and zero labelled negatives. An organization that has not bought Avocado has almost always simply never been asked. Details in § 7.

Model C — can Avocado occupy the technology slot without becoming the prime?

A transparent scorecard over clauses extracted from the solicitation PDF: does it permit subgrants, does it permit partnerships, is technology an allowable cost, does the applicant lack in-house build capacity. Deliberately not learned. There is no training signal — Avocado has one observed instance — and the inputs are legally load-bearing. A model that quietly learned "Idaho solicitations are fine" would be worse than useless.

How family D is actually computed

Nine offload roles — community health worker, home visitor, doula, lactation consultant, HealthySteps specialist, care coordinator, perinatal mental health, family resource navigator, parent educator — each with a weighted lexicon matched against job-posting titles and bodies, Form 990 Part III program narratives, and site copy. Source kind carries a weight: a posting title is stronger evidence than a 990 mention, because it implies a funded, currently open position.

Scores saturate as 1 − exp(−Σw) rather than accumulating linearly. What matters for Avocado is whether the role exists and is funded, not its headcount, and a saturating sum stops one large system with forty CHW postings from outranking a county coalition with two.

§ 4

Validation protocol

Forward chaining only, with as-of feature computation. This is the single most important engineering decision in the system and it is not a stylistic preference.

USAspending subaward records are backfilled by months — a July action date routinely first appears in September. A naive join lets the model see awards that had not been published when the shortlist would have been built, and produces a backtest that flatters itself by twenty to thirty NDCG points. Every feature here is computed at as_of = close_date − 60 days, and the loader refuses any record whose observation date is later.

What this table says, and why it is the most useful output here

In the first trained version, the staff-model family carried 49% of total split gain — by that measure the most important thing in the model. Removing it improved validation NDCG by +0.0232. That combination has one explanation: nine continuous, high-cardinality, nearly-noise features give a gradient-boosted tree an enormous number of plausible split points, so it memorises the training set through them and crowds out the award-history features that actually generalise.

The fix is architectural, not a regularisation dial. Staff model predicts whether an organization is a good Avocado deployment. It does not predict whether that organization wins a grant. Those are Model B and Model A, and feeding B's features to A degrades both. The families flagged here were moved rather than deleted — family D is now Model B's primary input, where it belongs. NDCG@20 went from 0.129 to 0.171 with 26 of 63 features retained.

A permutation-importance chart would have shown the same 49% and been read as confirmation that the Avocado-specific work was paying off. Only the ablation, run against a held-out fold, distinguishes a feature that carries signal from one that carries capacity.

§ 7

Model B: learning fit from five positives

Avocado has roughly five known deployments and no labelled negatives at all. Training a standard classifier on positives-versus-everything-else learns "unlabelled," not "bad fit," and will confidently reject good prospects that were simply never contacted.

Elkan–Noto handles this. Train a non-traditional classifier g(x) = P(s=1|x) on the labelled indicator, estimate the label frequency c = P(s=1|y=1) out of fold, and recover P(y=1|x) = g(x)/c.

157labelled positives of 4,059
0.039estimated label frequency c
0.609AUC vs latent fit (oracle 0.731)
0.78top-decile precision (base 0.60)

Two results worth stating precisely rather than glossing.

The PU correction does not improve ranking, and cannot. Dividing by a constant is a monotone transform, so AUC is identical by construction — 0.609 here against 0.605 for the uncorrected model, a difference that is fitting noise. What the correction fixes is magnitude. The naive model's mean predicted probability is 0.039, which is the labelling rate; it would tell leadership that almost no organization in the country is a fit. The corrected model recovers a prevalence near the true 0.60. Prevalence is what gets multiplied by dollars, so the correction matters for expected value and not at all for the shortlist order.

Calibration was the hard part, and the obvious tool was wrong. Scikit-learn's CalibratedClassifierCV averages the calibrated outputs of k fold-models. Under 4% positives the per-fold sigmoids are nearly flat, and averaging them destroyed the ranking the base model had found: AUC fell from 0.605 to 0.522. Replacing it with a single sigmoid fitted on pooled out-of-fold scores, applied to a base model refit on all data, restored the ranking and still calibrates honestly, because the sigmoid never sees a score its own base model produced in-sample.

The weakest number in this system

48% of organizations have g(x) > c and therefore cap at p_fit = 1.0. With 157 positives, c is estimated across a range of 0.0387 to 0.0388 and the fit probability stops discriminating at the top of the list. The consequence is specific and should be stated to anyone reading the output: expected value is a reliable ordering and an unreliable dollar forecast until the labelled set reaches roughly fifty real deployments. It is usable now for deciding who to call first; it is not usable now for forecasting a pipeline number.

The Elkan–Noto correction also assumes positives are Selected Completely At Random. That is approximately true today, since Avocado's known deployments came from inbound, personal networks and one RFP, none of which correlate strongly with the fit features. It stops being true the moment this model starts choosing who gets contacted. Re-estimate c every quarter and alarm if it moves more than 30% between refits, because a moving c is how that assumption announces it has failed.

§ 8

The failure mode worth building a test for

Small rural CBOs post fewer jobs, file thinner 990s, and frequently have no website. Award history, firmographics and staff-model evidence are all systematically sparser for them. An uncorrected ranker learns "we know less about them" and expresses it as "they win less."

That failure would point Avocado away from precisely the counties the product exists for. SMS reaches low-broadband, low-digital-literacy, rural families because an app does not. A model that quietly deprioritised frontier counties would be optimising against the company's own thesis, and it would do so invisibly.

§ 9

From probability to a week's work

Expected value is P(win) × P(fit) × P(role) × ACV × reach × discount. Deal size enters only through ACV and is not separately weighted, which mirrors the ×1 weight in the existing rubric.

Ranking purely by expected value puts state health departments and MCOs at the top of every list, because their ACV is five to twenty times a CBO's. Those are also the twelve-to-thirty-six month cycles. Run that way, the pipeline books no revenue in year one and starves the three-to-six month segments that fund the company — exactly the failure the ×1 weight was written to prevent, arrived at by a different route.

So the allocator carries the sequencing as an explicit constraint rather than hoping the weights hold it: at least 45% of the week from the NOW segments, at most 15% from LATER, no more than three targets per state. The objective is a sum of per-target values and the constraints form a partition matroid, so greedy selection is exact rather than approximate. This week's list comes out 9 NOW and 11 NEXT.

§ 10

What this does not do

  1. The numbers above are from simulation. State anchors, prime award IDs and obligation totals are real, taken from the 2026-09-03 briefing and the USAspending records it cites. The organization population and award outcomes are generated. Model quality here is evidence that the harness is sound and the metrics are honestly computed — it is not evidence of live accuracy, and no figure on this page should be repeated to a partner.
  2. Expected value is an ordering, not a forecast, until Model B has roughly fifty labelled deployments. See § 7.
  3. Partnership-graph features are unproven. They contribute zero gain in simulation for a structural reason that will not hold on live data. Re-check at first live training.
  4. Contact-level resolution is out of scope here. This ranks organizations. Turning an organization into three named humans with verified emails and a role classification is a separate pipeline, and it is the one that determines whether any of this converts.
  5. It will degrade the moment it succeeds. Once the model chooses who gets contacted, the SCAR assumption behind Model B breaks and the award labels start reflecting Avocado's own presence. Log every scored list with its timestamp so a future refit can condition on what was recommended, not only on what happened.
§ 11

Build order

Nothing here requires the whole system to exist before any of it is useful.

  1. Briefing parser and diff engine, one week — already written. Every Civic Operator edition becomes an immutable snapshot; deltas become triggers. A contact flipping from "Not published" to source-verified, a due date moving, a subaward cohort growing from 19 to 24 — each is an outreach event that is invisible if you only ever read today's edition. Zero machine learning, and it pays for itself the first time a deadline moves.
  2. Cascade graph and unobligated remainder, one week. USAspending subaward endpoints, the subtraction in § 1, one table. Answers the open question of which states to pilot in with a computed number.
  3. Model A on real award history, three to four weeks. Assistance listings 93.798, 93.505, 93.926, 93.994 and 93.224 give several thousand real labelled events. The protocol in § 4 runs unchanged.
  4. Solicitation document intelligence, two weeks. Clause extraction feeding Model C. Idaho's Laserfiche WebLink is scriptable; most state portals are.
  5. Model B, ongoing. Blocked on labels, not on code. Every closed-won and closed-lost outcome logged from today forward is a training example, which is a reason to instrument the CRM now rather than later.

What the parser found in the 2026-09-03 edition

Run against the real briefing, the parser reads 45 contact rows and 4 opportunities. Of those 45 rows, 6 carry a public source and 39 do not. Civic Operator is scrupulous about the distinction and marks every unsourced row, which is the discipline that makes the feed worth paying for — but it means roughly six in seven contacts in a given edition are candidates for a resolution pipeline rather than addresses to mail. Government email patterns are inferable and verifiable (firstname.lastname@ky.gov is unmistakable from the Kentucky block alone), so this is a solvable gap and a large one.

Writing that parser also surfaced a bug worth repeating because it is the kind that fails silently. The first version keyed rows on contact name. The 2026-09-03 edition contains the literal string "No named individual published" five times across five different agencies, so name-keyed rows collapse and every subsequent change to four of them becomes undetectable. The shipped version keys on (opportunity, name, organization) and raises a duplicate_key event rather than letting one row quietly win. Across this edition that recovers 3 rows that would otherwise have disappeared. A diff engine that misses changes is worse than no diff engine, because it is trusted.

Steps one and two are the ones to do first regardless of whether the models ever get built. They convert a subscription that prints a document into an asset that accumulates.
