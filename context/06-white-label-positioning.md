# 06 — White-Labeling & Platform Positioning

*Source: "Avocado Health White-Labeling Strategy and Long-Term DHCS Positioning," Health Management Associates, 8/12/2026. **Confidential.** Distilled with GTM implications.*

---

## 1. The strategic tension

The same white-labeling model that **accelerates short-term adoption** among providers, health plans, and CBOs can **limit long-term recognition** as a scalable statewide platform.

The question is **not** whether to offer white-labeling. It is **how much** white-labeling Avocado can offer before it loses the market visibility needed to become a recognized statewide solution and a potential future DHCS-supported platform.

**HMA's recommendation: a hybrid strategy** that maximizes adoption while preserving platform identity and cumulative evidence.

## 2. What stakeholder interviews found

Based on interviews with health plans, provider organizations, CHW stakeholders, and technology partners:

> Avocado's strongest market position is **not** as a standalone consumer-facing application, but as a **workflow-integrated care-team augmentation platform** that extends the reach of CHWs, care managers, HealthySteps Specialists, and other non-licensed support staff.

Stakeholders consistently valued Avocado's ability to:
- support documentation
- enhance engagement
- improve reimbursement opportunities
- integrate into existing care delivery workflows

**GTM implication — this is the most important line in the report.** It means the pitch is *care-team augmentation*, not *parent app*. Every message in `05` should be checkable against this. If a message sounds like it's selling a consumer product, it's off-position.

## 3. Why partners want white-labeling

Organizations that prefer it: First 5 programs, HealthySteps sites, FQHCs, Medicaid managed care plans, CBOs, county health departments.

They want families interacting with *"HealthySteps Family Support," "Parent Resource Line," "Community Health Worker Connect"* — not a third-party vendor brand.

For Avocado this buys:
- **Faster adoption** — orgs implement technology that reinforces their own brand
- **Greater trust** — families trust communications from organizations they already know
- **Easier workflow integration** — functions as an extension of an existing program
- **Better partner alignment** — seen as supporting the partner's objectives, not competing for visibility

## 4. The risk: invisibility, not non-adoption

> "The primary risk is not member adoption. The primary risk is becoming invisible."

If every deployment runs under a unique local brand, DHCS sees *HealthySteps LA*, *IEHP Parent Support*, *First 5 Orange Family Line*, *Health Net Family Connect*, *County CHW Engagement Platform* — without realizing they're all the same underlying technology. Avocado accumulates revenue and contracts but **not market recognition**.

Consequences:
- **Difficulty building statewide credibility** — DHCS sees disconnected programs instead of one scalable solution
- **Reduced thought leadership** — publications, pilot outcomes, and implementation wins attach to partners, not Avocado
- **Increased commoditization risk** — invisible vendors are easier to replace
- **Reduced leverage in future procurement** — recognized platforms get greater consideration when statewide initiatives emerge

## 5. Lessons from comparable platforms

**Healthcare**

| Company | Model | Key lesson | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unite Us** | Community resource referral network | Local orgs brand their own programs while Unite Us stays recognized as the infrastructure; every implementation strengthens one statewide/national network story | **High** |
| **Findhelp** (ex-Aunt Bertha) | Social care referral platform | Remains recognized by purchasers and policymakers even though consumers rarely interact with the brand | **High** |
| **Innovaccer** | Population health & analytics | Operates behind the scenes but is highly visible to health system execs, payers, and government. Industry recognition matters more than consumer recognition | High |
| **Xealth** | Workflow integration platform | Providers own the patient relationship; Xealth owns the technology narrative | High |

**Non-healthcare — the strongest analogies**

| Company | Insight |
|---|---|
| **Shopify** | Consumers identify with the merchant brand; the industry recognizes Shopify as the platform. Customers own front-end identity while Shopify accumulates platform value. |
| **Stripe** | Most consumers don't know Stripe processes their payment, yet Stripe is one of the most recognizable infrastructure companies in tech. **Visibility among decision-makers matters more than visibility among end users.** |
| **Twilio** | Millions of texts delivered without users knowing Twilio exists; Twilio still became the dominant communications infrastructure provider. → *Avocado may be positioned to become "the Twilio for healthcare engagement, navigation, and community health worker communication."* |

## 6. The recommended framework: three deployment tiers

| | **Tier 1: Avocado Branded** | **Tier 2: Co-Branded** ← *recommended default* | **Tier 3: Fully White-Labeled** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Use cases** | Demonstrations, conferences, pilot programs, direct sales, DHCS engagement, strategic partnerships | "HealthySteps Family Support **Powered by Avocado Health**" / "IEHP Family Connect **Powered by Avocado Health**" | Enterprise health plans; strategic customers; situations where platform branding creates procurement challenges |
| **Goal** | Build platform awareness and market identity | Maintain partner ownership while preserving Avocado recognition. **Should be the standard deployment model whenever possible.** | Offer selectively and **charge a premium**. Platform invisibility is a concession requiring compensation. |

**GTM implication:** "Powered by Avocado Health" is the default ask in every partnership conversation. Full invisibility is a paid concession, not a free courtesy. This should be a line item in commercial discussions, and Bhuvan should flag it whenever a prospect assumes full white-labeling is standard.

## 7. Implications for DHCS and statewide approval

If the long-term objective is becoming a recognized solution within California's Medicaid ecosystem, **branding alone will not determine success.** DHCS is more likely to evaluate:

demonstrated outcomes · adoption across providers and plans · scalability · workforce support capabilities · compliance · interoperability · data reporting · quality improvement impact · cost-effectiveness

**The strongest future narrative:**
> "Avocado powers family engagement, CHW support, care navigation, and between-visit communication across multiple health plans, provider organizations, and community programs serving thousands of California families."

That story only holds **if every implementation contributes to a common evidence base.**

**Strategic recommendation:** optimize for platform visibility among decision-makers while allowing flexibility in customer-facing branding.

## 8. Ideal future state — the balanced visibility model

| Members & Families | Providers & Plans | DHCS |
|---|---|---|
| Interact with trusted local brands: HealthySteps, First 5, IEHP, Health Net, pediatric clinic programs | Recognize Avocado as: the technology platform · the workflow enablement layer · the engagement infrastructure · the care-team augmentation tool · a scalable extension of human expert support | Sees one scalable statewide infrastructure platform: operating across organizations · supporting measurable outcomes · building a common evidence base |

**Core principle:**
> **Be invisible to the member when needed. Be highly visible to the healthcare ecosystem.**

## 9. Operational consequences for this project

1. **Data discipline is strategy.** Every deployment must feed one common evidence base. When scoping a partnership, always secure the right to report aggregate, de-identified outcomes — this is what compounds into DHCS credibility. Treat it as a deal term, not an afterthought.
2. **Tier 2 is the default ask.** Log the branding tier for every opportunity in the pipeline schema (`04 §7`) so leadership can see the visibility mix, not just revenue.
3. **Target decision-maker visibility, not consumer awareness.** Podcast guests, conference presence, and published pilot outcomes are pipeline activities, not marketing extras. This is why the podcast-first channel in `04 §3` is strategically coherent and not just a conversion tactic.
4. **The GTM study Hans requested** — analyze how Stripe, Shopify, Twilio and other infrastructure companies built partnerships, find the unifying threads, and translate them into an Avocado GTM framework with specific sales motions and outreach approaches — is a live deliverable. `07`, item A-4. This document is the starting input; the deliverable should extend to sales motions, partnership construction, and growth mechanics.
