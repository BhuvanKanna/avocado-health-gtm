# 05 — Messaging Playbook

*What to say, to whom, in what order. Pairs with `02` (who) and `04` (when).*

---

## 1. Message architecture

Every outreach follows the same four-beat structure. Beat 1 is segment-specific; beats 2–4 are largely reusable.

1. **Their trigger** — a dated, specific thing happening in *their* world (`02 §3`)
2. **The offloaded role** — the named staff role whose time Avocado gives back
3. **The proof** — one relevant, first-party proof point (`01 §5`)
4. **The ask** — a podcast conversation, not a demo (`04 §3`)

**Never lead with:** the AI, the product features, "24/7 support," or the mission story. Those are beats 5+ and only if asked. Lead with their money, their deadline, or their staffing problem.

## 2. Segment openers

### CBO — Parent Advocacy Orgs & Community-Based Orgs
> **Trigger:** grant renewal / RFP season; funder pressure to demonstrate AI-driven scale.
> **Open on:** "Your funders are asking how you're using AI to scale impact." This is the single most effective opener for this segment — the Advocacy Organizations page is built entirely around it.
> **Offloaded role:** home visitors, CHWs, hotline staff — 1–2 hours/day back.
> **Proof:** HMHB Hawaii — 40 monthly calls → 500+ touchpoints in month one, white-labeled under *their* brand, integrated with the state DOH.
> **Handle the objection early:** they fear tech replacing the human home-visitor relationship. Say plainly that Avocado handles low-acuity volume so their people can do the high-acuity relational work — and that the deployment carries *their* name, not Avocado's.

### OB — Perinatal & Maternal Health Networks
> **Trigger:** budget cuts to social workers and child-life specialists; rising maternal mental-health attention.
> **Offloaded role:** pediatric nurses, doulas, lactation consultants — 2–3 hours/day.
> **Proof:** the Riverside clinical cadence — gestational-week-anchored messaging built against the ACOG calendar, targeting hemorrhage, hypertensive disorders, gestational diabetes, and perinatal mental health at the specific weeks they present.
> **Differentiator to lead with:** the pregnancy-loss protocol — one-tap pause, no automated re-engagement, bereavement routing. This lands hard with perinatal professionals because it signals clinical seriousness that most vendors don't have.
> **Objection:** grant-funding fragility and integration with existing hotlines/warmlines. Answer: Avocado *sits behind* an existing warmline rather than replacing it — that's exactly the HMHB configuration.

### FQHC — Health Centers & Rural Clinics
> **Trigger:** **HR1 Medicaid redetermination, effective January 2027.** Lead with this, always.
> **Reframe:** this is a revenue-protection conversation, not a parenting conversation. 20–25% of their covered population is at risk; disenrolled patients are uncompensated patients. Avocado moves redetermination from a ~180-day process to ~10–15 minutes by text.
> **Second beat:** frees MAs and nurses 2–3 hours/day; lifts HEDIS measures (well-child visits, vaccinations).
> **Objection:** EHR integration and thin margins. Answer: SMS-only requires no EHR integration to start; there's a potential HCPCS reimbursement pathway; the redetermination case is a revenue argument, not a cost one.
> **Who to open with:** the **CFO** on the redetermination angle is often a faster door than the CMO.

### GOV — State & Local Public Health Departments
> **Trigger:** RHTP dollars flowing 2026–2027; Medicaid unwinding workload.
> **Frame:** "a fundable, deployable way to put rural-health and SDOH dollars to work quickly, with reportable outcomes and no new clinical headcount."
> **Proof:** the Hawaii DOH partnership.
> **Objection:** procurement complexity, HIPAA/security review, multi-year cycles. Answer: USFCR Verified Vendor / SAM-registered; HIPAA-compliant; and — critically — Avocado can deploy *through an existing grantee* rather than requiring a new direct procurement. That last point removes the biggest blocker.
> **Note:** at the state level, the near-term goal is usually not a contract. It's being known and being named in the plan. Calibrate the ask accordingly.

### RHS — Rural Hospitals & Health Systems
> **Trigger:** RHTP funding; rural OB-unit closures increasing need for remote support.
> **Offloaded role:** extends pediatric/OB reach into the home between visits.
> **Lead with the IT footprint:** they have limited IT/security bandwidth. "No app, no portal, no EHR integration required to start" is a stronger opener here than anywhere else.
> **Budget path:** community-benefit dollars, not the operating budget.
> **Objection:** thin margins, competing capital priorities. Answer: reduces preventable ER visits and NICU stays; supports retention of clinician-parents on staff.

### ABA — Behavioral Health / Autism Therapy Clinics
> **Trigger:** staff turnover and burnout; PE roll-up consolidation seeking standardized care.
> **Fastest cycle in the matrix (1–3 months), owner-operator decision.** Short, direct outreach works here — this is the one segment where the podcast detour may be unnecessary.
> **Proof:** the BCBA who owns 12 clinics.
> **Non-negotiable framing:** an *enhancement to*, never a replacement for, billable parent-training codes. Getting this wrong kills the deal instantly.
> **Value:** marketing differentiator for clinics competing for families; better at-home strategy consistency; admin relief for BCBAs, RBTs, front desk.

### MCO — Medicaid Health Plans & Payers
> **Trigger:** state redetermination mandates; CMS SDOH/health-equity measures; rising NICU/ER cost trend.
> **Frame:** the "$500B Cost of Not Knowing" — preventable preterm births, NICU stays, ER overuse.
> **Language:** MLR, HEDIS, avoidable utilization, no-app member engagement.
> **Reality check:** 9–36 months, requires actuarial-grade ROI. **Warm intro only.** Do not run cold outbound into this segment; it consumes effort that belongs in the 3–6 month segments.

### HR — Self-Funded Employers *(confirm ownership before working — `07` O-5)*
> **Trigger:** Q3/Q4 open-enrollment planning; rising claims costs.
> **Numbers:** 60% of the workforce are working parents; ~$8K avoided cost per employee at a claimed 10–15x ROI; presenteeism ≈ 15% of salary cost.
> **Objection:** point-solution fatigue and broker gatekeeping. The structural answer is to go *through* brokers (segment BRK) rather than around them — one broker relationship can unlock 10+ employer accounts.

### Foundations *(connector, not customer)*
> Never pitch. Ask what they're seeing across their grantee portfolio, and what they wish grantees could do. The goal is a referral into their portfolio and, ideally, for them to keep asking grantees the AI question. A foundation program officer who mentions Avocado to five grantees is worth more than any single deal.

## 3. Outreach templates

### A. Podcast invitation (primary channel)
```
Subject: [Their org] on the Parent Confidence Lab?

Hi [Name],

I came across [specific thing — their county's EII findings / their
home-visiting expansion / their testimony on the state RHT plan] and
wanted to reach out.

I work with Avocado Health — we run a podcast called the Parent
Confidence Lab, where we talk with people building maternal and child
health infrastructure about what's actually working. [Specific reason
they'd be a great guest — a program they run, a result they published,
a position they hold.]

Would you be open to a conversation? Happy to work around your
schedule, and we handle everything on our end.

[Name]
```
**Why this works:** offers visibility instead of requesting time; positions them as expert; creates a legitimate follow-up reason regardless of outcome; produces content that seeds the foundation and thought-leader network.

### B. Warm intro request (to the connector, not the target)
```
Hi [Connector],

Quick ask — I'm trying to reach [Name] at [Org]. [One sentence on why
they're relevant: their state's RHT plan names maternal health and
they're likely to apply for subawards this cycle.]

Would you be comfortable making an intro? Happy to send a forwardable
blurb so it's zero effort on your end.
```
Always include the forwardable blurb. Never make the connector write it.

### C. Cold email — last resort, hard trigger only
```
Subject: [Their state]'s RHT maternal health allocation

[Name] — [dated, specific, verifiable trigger. e.g., "Your state's
approved RHT plan names maternal and child health as a priority area,
and subaward applications close in March."]

We're the text-based parent support platform behind Healthy Mothers
Healthy Babies' Parent Line in Hawaii — white-labeled under their
brand, funded through a Department of Health award. In the first month
their monthly touchpoints went from about 40 to over 500.

If [Org] is putting an application together, it may be worth a
conversation about whether that kind of layer strengthens it.

Worth 20 minutes?
```
Rules: one specific trigger, one proof point, one ask. No feature lists. No mission story. Under 120 words.

## 4. Proof-point selection guide

| If they care about… | Use |
|---|---|
| Winning funding | Hawaii: HMHB won a ~$1M/yr DOH RFP with Avocado as the layer underneath |
| Staff capacity | 1–2 hrs/day back for CHWs; 2–3 hrs/day for nurses, doulas, lactation consultants |
| Engagement / will parents actually use it | 40 → 500+ touchpoints in month one; 75%+ active engagement; 90% read within 20 min |
| Cost | <$4/month per active covered family |
| Clinical seriousness | Gestational-week ACOG-anchored cadence; pregnancy-loss pause protocol; red-flag escalation |
| Equity / access | No app, no broadband, works on any phone; multilingual bridging; 43% of Riverside speaks another language at home |
| Their own brand | Fully white-labeled under the partner's name (`06`) |
| Evidence base | The four published SMS-in-pregnancy studies in `01 §5` — and note Avocado is two-way where those were one-way |

## 5. Hard rules

1. **No medical claims.** Evidence-based information, not diagnosis. See `01 §6`.
2. **No confidential material outbound.** The ICP matrix, HMA report, deal economics, Riverside brief, and partner terms are MNDA-covered. Public-site facts and the Hawaii figures Hans has already used externally are fine.
3. **Prefer first-party Hawaii figures** over site-published generic stats where they conflict (`01 §5`).
4. **The partner gets the credit.** Every message should make the recipient the hero of the outcome. That is not modesty — it is the mechanism that won Hawaii.
5. **One ask per message.**
6. **Never pitch a foundation.**
